Marine ecology & biogeochemistry

Jaebin JangPh.D.

I study how environmental change shapes planktonic food webs in estuaries, coastal waters, and continental shelves. My research connects phytoplankton production and nutrient uptake with grazing, food sources, and trophic transfer.

I combine field observations, incubation experiments, stable isotopes, and statistical analysis to understand marine ecosystems, with a focus on the South Sea of Korea and the northern East China Sea.

Methods & experience

Field observations & incubations

Oceanographic surveys, seawater and plankton sampling, phytoplankton cultures, ¹³C primary production, ¹⁵N nitrate and ammonium uptake, dilution experiments, and mesozooplankton grazing.

Stable isotopes & fatty acids

EA-IRMS, biological and particulate sample preparation, fatty-acid extraction, FAME derivatization, and gas chromatography. Fatty-acid analyses contributed to Seo et al. (2023).

Plankton & biogeochemistry

Genus-level phytoplankton microscopy with Lugol fixation and Utermöhl sedimentation, ciliate and rotifer identification, HPLC pigments, CHEMTAX, chlorophyll a, nutrients, and particulate organic matter.

Reproducible analysis in R

GAMs (mgcv), regression, cross-validation, PCA, CCA, PERMANOVA, community and time-series analysis, Bayesian isotope mixing models (simmr), and isotopic niche analysis (SIBER).

Collaborative ecosystem modeling

Contributions to and interpretation of ROMS-based physical-biological and NPZD modeling for aquaculture carrying-capacity assessment. Additional software: PRIMER, SPSS, and SigmaPlot.

Mentoring & research practice

Regular experimental guidance and practical R analysis support for students and junior colleagues throughout doctoral and postdoctoral research.
